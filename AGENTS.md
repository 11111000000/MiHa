# AGENTS.md — MiHa

**MiHa** is a very small, embeddable harness for a decentralised agent: it runs in a
browser, hands work to a backend peer and takes it back, and it is written to be an
example rather than merely to work.

The full decision record is in `docs/adr/`; the route through the work is
`docs/plan.md`; how the project is developed is `docs/method.md`. Read those before
changing anything.

## The rules of this repository

1. **A change is a claim before it is code.** If a change is supposed to establish
   something, declare that something as a math-coding claim *before* writing it. The
   claim names its subject, its falsifier, the machine that checks it, and a control
   that provably breaks the check.
2. **A claim without a control is not a claim.** Every verifier must have a negative
   control that changes the subject and makes the verifier fail. If you cannot write
   one, you have not found the check yet — you have found a wish.
3. **No green without a fresh run.** `mathc run` decides, and the decision lives in
   `.math/v5/`, which is regenerated by the run and never edited by hand.
4. **The evidence trail is not content.** `.math/` is gitignored; `spec.json` is
   committed, because it is the specification, not the evidence.
5. **A changed decision is reviewed, not re-declared.** After an ADR changes, run
   `mathc re-evaluate`, then `mathc review-source ID --by <owner> --reason "<why>"`,
   then `mathc run` again. Only a person may record a review, and only for a decision
   they actually re-read.
6. **Do not weaken a verifier to get green.** A failing check is information. Fix the
   subject, or change the claim — and changing the claim means saying out loud what
   changed and why.
7. **Budgets are laws.** The bundle ceiling, the layer budgets, the dependency count
   and the absence of `any` are enforced by `scripts/check-budget.mjs`. Exceeding one
   means a module is split or a feature is cut — not that the budget is raised.
8. **Every law states its derivation.** A law with no derivation is a preference
   wearing the name of a law. Say where it comes from: a fact about the provider
   protocols, or a policy we chose.
9. **Our own harness, our own decisions.** Upstream pi is a source of *questions*, not
   of answers. Where our behaviour differs from another's, the difference is stated and
   justified.
10. **A claim is settled only after a discriminator has tried to break it.** A control
    proves the verifier is sensitive to one mutation. It does not prove the claim is
    true.

## For the agents

Two roles, and they are not the same job.

**An implementer** writes the subject and its tests, and declares the claims. It never
edits `spec.json` and never edits a decision — it proposes, the owner records.

**A discriminator** tries to break the work. It reads the claim, the subject and the
verifier, invents mutations the claim's text forbids but the claim's own control does
not make, and tests them in an isolated copy. It reports; it never edits the subject and
never commits. The protocol is in [docs/discriminator.md](docs/discriminator.md).

Work runs in parallel when the **claim scopes do not overlap** — one file, one agent —
and the core is frozen while the layers above it are built. At most two heavy checks at
a time: this host has four cores and several agents live on it.

## The loop

```sh
bun run check        # the gate: budget, documents, types, tests, evidence
mathc next           # what to do next, and why
mathc map            # the verdict, the states, the uncovered paths
```

Before a slice: `mathc add ID "<what must be true>" "<subject>" "<what would refute it>" \
fact --requirement "verifier:<command>" --control "<command that breaks it>"`.

After a slice: `bun run check`, then commit. The commit is not made until the gate is
green.
